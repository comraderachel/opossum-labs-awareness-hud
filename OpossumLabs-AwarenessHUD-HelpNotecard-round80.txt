1. WHAT THIS HUD DOES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Wear it and it quietly watches the parcel or region you are in
(or a radius around you) for avatars coming and going. When
someone new arrives, you get a private message with their
account age, payment status, and viewer language. You can also
keep a watch list of specific people and be told when they enter
or leave the region.

Only YOU see any of this: nothing is said out loud and nobody
nearby can tell you are wearing it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. FIRST-TIME SETUP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The first time you wear this HUD - and again after a Wipe or a
change of owner - it greets you a few seconds after it boots and
asks where you want it to watch (Parcel / Region / Radius, then
a size if you pick Radius). The dialog suggests Parcel, but you
can pick whichever you prefer. Clicking Ignore keeps the defaults
(Parcel, monitoring ON) and it will not ask again.

NOTE: In very busy areas (clubs, big events), Region scope can
only track the first few dozen arrivals - the exact number adapts
to how much memory the HUD has free - so Parcel is usually the
better choice there.

The HUD also seats itself on your screen: the first time you
wear it, and each time you move it to a DIFFERENT HUD position,
it hops to a sensible spot for that position so it is never
stuck half off the screen. Its designed home is the TOP RIGHT
HUD position - attach it there for the neatest fit. After the
first seating it leaves you alone - drag it wherever you like,
and the viewer remembers your position for that HUD position.
(If you attach it to a body part instead of a HUD position, it
warns you and stays put until you attach it properly.)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT THE BUTTONS DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Power Button: GREEN means monitoring is ON. Click it to pause
  (it turns RED). While OFF, the HUD is in true standby: it
  does no region counting at all, and Summary, List All and
  Watch+ politely refuse until you turn it back on - just
  click the power button again. Everything else (Status,
  Debug, Manual Add, Watch-, Watch List, Export, Import) keeps
  working while it is off.
  While the power light BLINKS, the HUD is fetching account
  data (a List All you just clicked, or the one-line summary
  after arriving in a region) - that is it thinking, not a
  fault.

Help Button: gives you this notecard.

Sound Button: turns the alert "ping" sound on and off. GREEN
  means the ping is on, RED means it is off (the same colors
  as the power button).

Menu Button: opens the settings menu. This is the ONLY part of
  the panel that opens the menu; clicking the panel body does
  nothing (that is on purpose).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE MENU
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
MAIN MENU (frequently used):

Summary: prints the one-line summary you also get when entering
  a region (avatars in scope, scripted agents, new accounts,
  people on your watch list), any time you want it. It works
  in any region - even one where a busy sim has detection
  paused - because it counts everyone in scope fresh, the
  same way List All does. In a busy region the line also
  tells you how many people the memory cap is holding back
  ("detection tracking the first N"), so you get the full
  picture on demand.
List All: lists the people currently in scope - up to 20,
  never yourself - with the same
  details as the arrival messages, plus how far away from
  you they were when the list was made (e.g. " | 25m away").
  Lines appear as each person's account data arrives, and
  the power button blinks while fetching. The list is made
  fresh from the region itself by the HUD's listing script,
  so it also works in regions where a busy sim has detection
  paused - the list is everyone in scope NOW. A preamble
  notes when a busy-region cap is in effect.
Watch+: add someone to your watch list (see section 5).
Watch-: remove someone from your watch list.
Watch List: show your watch list (each entry shows the avatar's
  key next to the name).
Encounters: shows where and when you have run into people on
  your watch list, plus automated agents (see section 6).
Status: quick summary of on/off, Area (where the HUD is
  watching), watch count, and whether the Data Script is
  still running. The line ends with the build number
  (e.g. "Build:110126080") - quote it when reporting an
  issue. (Memory is NOT here
  anymore - use Settings > Debug > Status Check for that.)
Settings: opens the settings submenu (below).

Things the panel already has buttons for (Power, Help, and the
Sound ping) are not repeated in the menus.

SETTINGS SUBMENU:

Area: where and how wide the HUD watches, in two steps.
  Step 1 asks WHERE:
  - Parcel: the parcel you are standing on. This is the
    default for a new HUD and the suggested choice.
  - Region: the whole region you are in. In very busy areas
    it can only track the first few dozen arrivals (the
    number adapts to free memory).
  - Radius: a sphere around you that travels with you.
  Whichever you pick, the watch list is always checked against
  the whole region - the Area setting only narrows the ordinary
  arrival notices.
  If you pick Radius, step 2 asks HOW WIDE: 10m, 25m, 50m,
  100m, or Custom (a pop-up text box). Nothing changes until
  you pick a size - if you press Back instead, your scope stays
  as it was.
  In Parcel scope, walking into a new parcel is silent - you
  only hear about people who arrive after you.
Export: sends your watch list to chat so you can move it to
  another copy of this HUD (a replacement unit after an
  update, a fresh backup). The list prints in your chat AND is
  announced on a private transfer channel - see Import below.
  Both the HUD you export FROM and the HUD you import INTO
  must be worn by the same you (same owner).
Import: listens for an Export from another copy of the HUD for
  60 seconds. Wear both copies, click Settings > Export on the
  old one, then Settings > Import on the new one - the watch
  list transfers across, one entry at a time. Entries that are
  already on the list are quietly skipped, the list is capped
  at 20, and nothing else (settings, encounters) is copied -
  the import window closes by itself after 60 seconds.
Encounters: shows how long the encounter history is kept -
  30 days, fixed. Older entries are removed automatically
  (see section 6).
Debug: opens the diagnostics submenu:
  Status Check: a troubleshooting report with the HUD
  version, your current settings, the private chat channel,
  the linkset-data space, and one memory line for each of
  the four scripts, each as "N free (P% used) | Build:
  110126080" - the build number on each line tells you
  which build EACH script is running (they should all
  match). The
  four memory lines can appear in any order - but a script
  that has halted shows NO line at all, and that gap is
  itself the diagnosis. The report also lists recent known errors,
  such as account-data lookups that got no reply from the
  servers (a small rotating log that Wipe clears), and
  history notes about freezes: a tally of times the HUD
  could not run while worn ("starved the HUD N time(s),
  longest Xs") and any stretch of 5 minutes or more with no
  detection pass at all. With Debug Log also on, the report
  ends with the listing script's recorded events - its boot
  number and any refused or interrupted listings. They
  record times the sim was too
  busy to schedule the HUD, or it was detached, or you were
  logged out - notes for the curious, not errors.
  Debug Log: turns event recording on or off (the dialog
  shows the current state). While on, the scripts record
  their important moments to a rotating on-HUD log - boot
  memory numbers, pipeline gaps, valve trips with the
  memory numbers at the moment of pausing, and the paused
  probe's before/after readings; the listing script records
  its boot number and any refused or interrupted listings.
  Leave it off normally;
  recording costs a little script memory.
  Dump Log: prints the recorded log to your chat, oldest
  first - copy the block whole when reporting an issue.
  (The listing script's story prints with Status Check while
  Debug Log is on, so one Status Check captures everything.)
Wipe: erase ALL saved settings, the watch list and the
  troubleshooting history (the error log and the freeze and
  gap records that Status Check reports), then reset all
  four scripts (you will see the boot lines again, plus
  one arrival summary - and the first-time welcome will appear
  again a few seconds later). If a script halted with an error,
  Wipe cannot restart it - take the HUD off and wear it again.
Back: go back one level - from the Radius size dialog back to
  Area, from Area back to Settings, from Settings back to
  the main menu.

POP-UP TEXT BOXES:
A few HUD actions (Manual Add for a watch-list key, Custom for
the radius size) open a small text box: type your answer, then
click "Submit". If you close it with "Ignore", nothing happens -
just try again. People nearby never see what you type, and
every watch-list name is added by clicking a button.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE WATCH LIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Adding someone: click Watch+ in the menu. It lists the people
closest to you (nearest first) as buttons - click a name and
they're on the list. Need someone who is not nearby, or not
online right now? Click "Manual Add" instead: a small text box
pops up; paste the avatar key (UUID) into the box and click
"Submit". (Adding someone who is already on the list just gets
a friendly "Already watching" message - no duplicates.)

An avatar key looks like this:

> 12345678-9abc-def0-1234-567890abcdef

Where to get a key: ask the person for their avatar key (many
residents know theirs), or copy it from the Watch List (the
menu's Watch List button), which shows each key next to the
name.

Tips:
- If you close the pop-up with "Ignore", nothing happens.
  Just click Watch+ again.
- Pressing Enter in the box works too (or Tab, then Enter).
- Watch- opens a dialog listing your entries as clickable
  name links, numbered, six per page - click the number
  button that matches the name to remove that entry (the
  "More" button flips to the next page). No typing needed.
- Moving your list to another copy of the HUD? Use
  Settings > Export on the old one and Settings > Import on
  the new one (both worn by you) - see section 4.
- Watched people are announced when they enter or leave the
  region you are in; ALWAYS region-wide, no matter which Area
  you picked (Parcel, Region, or Radius). A smaller Area only
  narrows the ordinary arrival notices; the watch list always
  covers the whole region.
- One notice, not two: a watched person entering or leaving
  INSIDE your Area gets the ordinary ARRIVED or LEFT line
  with [WATCHED] - no separate "entered/left the region"
  line. The "entered/left the region" lines only appear for
  watch-list people who are outside your Area.
- If YOU enter a region where someone on your list already is,
  the HUD tells you right away:

> WATCHED: Name is already in the region.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. ENCOUNTERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The HUD keeps a short history of meetings with people on your
watch list and with automated agents (scripted agents) - both
directions: when they enter your Area, and when you arrive
somewhere they already are. Click Encounters in the menu to see
it. Each entry shows the date and time (SLT - Second Life time),
the person, the region, and the parcel they were on:

> 2026-10-28 19:57 SLT - Rachel Frost - Lusk (Boardwalk)

Automated agents get a tag after their name:

> 2026-10-28 19:57 SLT - OpalBot Resident [AUTOMATED
> AGENT] - Lusk (Boardwalk)

- Watch-list people are logged region-wide, like their alerts;
  automated agents are logged within your Area, like their
  ARRIVED lines (in very busy regions the usual tracking cap
  applies).
- The history is kept for 30 days - a fixed window that needs
  no setting.
- The log holds at most 50 entries. When it is full the oldest
  is dropped first, and entries past their keep-time are
  removed when you view the log and at boot - so the storage
  can never overflow.
- Meeting the same person again in the same region within 24
  hours is not logged twice (relogs do not fill the log).
- Wipe erases the history, and it is cleared for a new owner
  along with the watch list.

7. UNDERSTANDING THE ALERTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
A typical arrival line:

> ARRIVED (region): liplip Criss | Age:17y 276d |
> Pay:None | Lang:Japanese | 25m away

If the arrival is a scripted agent (a bot), the line LEADS with
the marker instead of hiding it at the end:

> ARRIVED (region): AUTOMATED AGENT: SomeBot | Age:2d |
> Pay:None | Lang:English

Any tags (see below) close the line, after the distance:

> ARRIVED (region): MayaFour | Age:1d | Pay:None |
> Lang:Polish | 22m away [NEW ACCOUNT]

Names in alert lines appear as clickable links showing the
person's display name - click one to open their profile. The
links are resolved by your viewer itself, so they always show
the current display name, even for people who have already
left. (The small dialog buttons cannot show links, so those use
trimmed display names or numbers instead.)

- Age reads as days within the first year ("Age:123d") and
  switches to a friendlier form past it ("Age:2y 34d" means 2
  years and 34 days).
- The distance ("25m away") is how far they were
  from you when the HUD announced them. It is a snapshot -
  they may have moved since. If they left before the
  announcement was ready, no distance is shown.
- Pay is their payment status (the short forms mean the same
  as the viewer's payment info wording). You may see:
    None (no payment info on file)
    On File
    On File & Used
    Previously Used
    Unavailable (could not be read)
- Lang is the language their viewer is set to.

Other messages you may see:

> LEFT (region): Name
(The person left your scope. If they are on your watch list,
[WATCHED] is added.)

> WATCHED: Name entered the region.
> WATCHED: Name left the region.
(These fire only for watch-list people outside your scope. A
watched person inside your scope gets ONE notice - the
ARRIVED or LEFT line with [WATCHED].)

> WATCHED: Name is already in the region.
(When you arrive where they already are.)

> REGION SLOWDOWN: the sim is running at NN% of normal speed.
(Something heavy is running somewhere in the region - script
or particle spam, physics, or sim trouble. A "Region speed
recovered" line follows when it clears up.)

> Scanning was paused for 10m - usually a parcel with
> scripts disabled (the HUD cannot run there) or a sim too
> busy to run it. Scanning has resumed.
(Explains a silent stretch AFTER the fact. Some parcels have
scripts disabled: while you stand there, Second Life stops
every script in the HUD, so it cannot speak up until you
leave. An overloaded sim can stall it too. The line arrives
the moment the HUD is running again - see section 9.)

> Detection resumed after about N minute(s) - the HUD is ON
> and a region summary follows.
(After you log back in wearing the HUD (or it could not run
for five minutes or more), it says this once and then prints
the usual region summary, so you know it is awake and working
again.)

Tags and markers you may see:

AUTOMATED AGENT: at the START of a line means a scripted
  agent (a bot) - it leads the line so you cannot miss it.
[NEW ACCOUNT]: the account is less than 7 days old.
[WATCHED]: the person is on your watch list.
(The two bracketed tags close the line, after the distance.)

The Pay: field itself already shows "None" when there is no
payment info, so there is no extra tag for it, but it still
counts as a flag for the ping sound.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. THE PING SOUND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
For the Ping setting to make a sound, the HUD must contain a
sound item named exactly:

> notification

Your HUD already ships with a "notification" sound inside, so
the ping works out of the box. Want a different alert sound?
The HUD object is modify: open the HUD's contents, remove the
sound named "notification", put in any sound you like, and
rename it "notification". On a HUD, sounds are heard only by
you.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. TRAVELING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The HUD follows you. When you cross a region border or teleport,
it quietly resets its baseline. Instead of flooding you with
"arrived" messages for everyone already there, it prints ONE
summary line: the total number of avatars in scope, how many are
flagged as scripted agents, how many are new accounts, and how
many are on your watch list. In very busy areas the HUD tracks
only the first few dozen people - the number adapts to free
memory - and you see that note ONCE when you arrive ("Very busy
region - detection tracking the first N"), not repeatedly.
Summary and List All remind you of the cap on demand whenever
it is in effect. Your watch list is always checked against
everyone in the region, no matter how busy it is. You only hear
full details about people who arrive AFTER you do. (While the
summary is being prepared, the power button blinks; if a fetch
ever gets stuck, the blink stops by itself after 3 minutes.)

If script memory runs very low in a busy region, arrival and
watch alerts pause (one line tells you so) and come back on
their own as the region quiets down - the pause protects the
HUD from crashing. The summary keeps working for a quick
count. Settings > Debug > Status Check shows the
live memory numbers.

Some parcels have SCRIPTS DISABLED (welcome areas, infohubs,
and some public sandboxes are set up that way). Second Life
stops every script in the HUD while you stand there: no text,
no menus, nothing at all - the reset option cannot work
either, because no script is running to receive it. That is
normal behavior for attached scripts, not a fault with the
HUD. Walk or fly into a neighboring parcel that allows
scripts and the HUD wakes up by itself, prints the
"Scanning was paused" line to explain the quiet stretch, and
carries on where it left off. (Nothing is lost; detection
simply resumes. If the quiet stretch was five minutes or
more - like a logout - the HUD says "Detection resumed"
first, then prints a fresh summary.)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. SETTINGS AND MEMORY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Your settings and watch list are saved inside the HUD object
itself and survive script resets. "Wipe" in the menu erases
everything and restores the defaults.

To move your watch list to another copy of the HUD (for
example a replacement unit after an update), use
Settings > Export on the old copy and Settings > Import on
the new one - both worn by you at the same time. Encounters
and other settings do not transfer; the watch list is the
migration.

NOTE: If your viewer crashes, the very last changes may not
have been saved yet.

If you sell or give the HUD to someone else, your personal
watch list is cleared automatically for the new owner.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. MAKING CHANGES TO THE HUD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The HUD object itself is modify, so you can customize it - but
the scripts inside find the parts BY NAME. Renaming any prim,
unlinking the prims, or deleting one will break the HUD (the
buttons stop working, or detection stops). The scripts are
locked (no-modify), so that wiring cannot be fixed from the
outside. The HUD holds four scripts - Core Script (root
prim), Companion Script, Data Script and Registrar Script -
and the Wipe button finds the last three BY their exact
inventory names.

Before you make any changes like these, make a copy of the
HUD in your inventory first (right-click it and choose Copy, or
press Ctrl+Shift+C with it selected). That way you can always
go back to the working original if something stops working.

Safe to do at any time: moving the HUD around your screen - the
viewer remembers the position, and the HUD never fights you
for it. (The one thing it does on its own: if you MOVE it to a
different HUD position - say from bottom-left to top-right -
it seats itself once for that new position, then leaves you
alone again.)

12. TROUBLESHOOTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Help Button says no notecard found.
   The HUD must contain a notecard named exactly
   "Awareness Hud Help". Capital letters matter!

2. Menu does not open.
   The menu prim must be named exactly "Menu Button" in the
   Edit window.

3. A button does nothing.
   The four button prims must be named exactly: Power Button,
   Help Button, Sound Button, and Menu Button.

4. No ping sound.
   Check that a sound named "notification" is in the HUD
   contents, and that Ping is ON (Sound Button green, not
   red).

5. The HUD is partly or fully off the screen.
   Chat the reseat command on the HUD's private channel and
   it hops back to its default seat on the right edge. The
   channel is printed in the boot greeting (look for the
   "reseat" hint at the end of the boot line) and in Status
   Check. The command is the channel number, a space, then
   the word reseat:

   > /-1234567890 reseat

   (your channel number will differ). Re-attaching the HUD to
   the Top Right HUD position does the same thing.

6. The whole HUD went silent: buttons do nothing and there
   is no text at all. (That is different from the blinking
   power light, which just means the HUD is fetching data.)
   First check WHERE you are standing: a parcel with
   scripts disabled (welcome areas, infohubs, some public
   sandboxes) stops every script in the HUD - no text, no
   menus, and the reset option cannot work either. That is
   normal Second Life behavior, not a fault. Walk or fly
   into a parcel that allows scripts and the HUD wakes up
   by itself, explains the pause, and carries on.
   If you are NOT in a scripts-disabled parcel, it is a
   known Second Life scheduling glitch, not a problem with
   the HUD or your settings. Try these in order:
   - Cross into one more region, or teleport somewhere.
     That usually wakes it up.
   - Still silent? Take the HUD off, rez it on the ground,
     take it back into your inventory, and wear it again.
   - Still silent? Wear a fresh copy from your inventory
     (the backup copy you made before making changes).
   Once it wakes up it catches up on its own, and no
   settings are lost.
